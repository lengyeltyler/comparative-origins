# Methodology

Start with a surviving object, manuscript or explicitly identified edition. Record the exact passage or museum designation before assigning motifs or proposing relationships.

## Five independent categories

- **primary-evidence:** a report of what a source says/shows. Identify whether it was examined as an object, photograph, diplomatic text, composite edition or translation.
- **textual-reconstruction:** the editor’s intervention, restored text, join, lacuna or witness comparison. Do not silently present reconstruction as preserved wording.
- **historical-interpretation:** an attributed argument from philology, history or archaeology. A museum’s interpretation is not the object itself.
- **traditional-interpretation:** a particular tradition’s reception, dated independently of the text it interprets.
- **speculation:** a testable possibility without sufficient demonstration. State what evidence would change the assessment.

No category decides whether divine beings exist or an ancient supernatural report happened. Record unusual claims accurately. Keep historical corroboration separate. Do not supply invented negative claims such as “archaeology proves this never happened.”

## Units of comparison

Compare bounded passages, not timeless cultures. A tradition dossier can group sources, but it must not assign one date to all contents. Egyptian “theologies” are modern organizing labels; a dossier is not a claim that one continuous ancient epic survives.

Distinguish exact wording, original-language semantic fields, narrative sequence, genre, function and scholarly motif tagging. A word translated “chaos” is not automatically identical to Greek Chaos; dust is not automatically clay; a wand is not automatically a royal scepter.

The initial motif matrix has three positive kinds of entry: explicit primary attestation, interpretive association, and occurrence reported through scholarship. A missing entry is a statement about database coverage. This edition does not encode universal absence claims.

## Chronology

Keep four records: claimed setting, composition estimate, selected surviving manuscript date, and earliest known physical witness. Each numerical range requires a source, scope and confidence. Negative integers denote BCE, positive integers CE; year zero is disallowed. Approximate dates remain labeled approximate.

A broad corpus range is a context, not a date for each constituent spell. It is excluded from individual-witness timelines by default. The earliest entry in a growing database cannot establish earliest attestation. Uncertain or unreviewed fields are TODO, with null numeric dates.

## Relationships

Classify each proposal as direct dependence, shared tradition, cultural transmission, typological parallel, weak parallel, no known connection, disputed or unknown. Explain confidence, chronology, sources and counterevidence. “No known connection” is not proof of independence. “Direct dependence” requires distinctive textual evidence and a defensible route, not merely matching motif labels.

Do not count copies of a shared source as independent corroboration. Test motif clusters against base rates and negative controls. Cultural contact establishes an opportunity for exchange; each proposed transferred text or idea needs an independent argument. The contact register is not a genealogical tree.

## Validation and review

Software checks structure, category enums, citation identifiers, required locators, references, chronology intervals and the handling of unreviewed readings. It cannot verify a translation, dating proposal, provenance claim or consensus. Human reviewers should examine the cited passage and record their identity, review date and correction history before promoting an initial dossier to reviewed status.

The seven research dossiers remain open research work. Their investigative criteria are proposals, not historical discoveries.
