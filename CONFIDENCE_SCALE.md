# Confidence scale

Confidence belongs to an individual proposition. It is not a numeric probability or a statement about whether supernatural events are possible.

| Level | Use |
|---|---|
| Very high | Direct, unambiguous and corroborated evidence; justify the corroboration. |
| High | Clear support in the identified, checked source. |
| Moderate | A reasoned but contestable reading, dating or relationship. |
| Low | Limited or ambiguous support, substantial alternatives. |
| Speculative | An interesting possibility without a demonstrated chain. |
| Unknown | Unreviewed or insufficient evidence; no score assigned. |

A high-confidence primary-evidence claim means the text clearly says it, not that the described event has been historically demonstrated.

Visual labels are independent of confidence: PRIMARY SOURCE, SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS, MAJORITY VIEW, DISPUTED, MINORITY VIEW, SPECULATION, UNKNOWN; additional labels identify scholarly and traditional interpretation. Consensus/majority/minority labels require documentation about scholarly positions. The present validator rejects them until a suitable survey field and review workflow are implemented. Disputed does not mean equally supported alternatives.
