# Source policy

Prefer surviving primary evidence, critical editions, university and museum records, archaeological publications, peer-reviewed scholarship and major academic references, in that order as appropriate to the claim. Wikipedia may aid navigation but does not support any initial corpus assertion.

## A citation is more than a link

Each substantive comparison field includes a source identifier and passage, page, section or catalogue-field locator. The source register records title, author/institution, edition, source kind, access date, checked/discovery status, limitations and reuse notes. Stable identifiers are preserved, including museum numbers and manuscript sigla when verified.

A `reviewed` source means the selected material was checked during this build, not that every page or every manuscript was collated. `discovery-only` sources may appear in bibliographies but cannot substantiate a claim. HTTP availability is a separate diagnostic: 200 does not prove a reading; 403 or a timeout does not refute a citation.

Each ancient-language field needs a citation. Do not reconstruct original wording from an English translation. Preserve supplied diacritics; distinguish a normalized reading edition from a diplomatic transcription. Do not assign all Dead Sea Scrolls or all Septuagint manuscripts a single reading or date. Identify verse coverage before using a witness.

## Editions and research scope

Some initial passages use older translations. Their age makes neither their readings nor their interpretations authoritative. In particular, the Enuma Elish dossier flags Heidel’s obsolete IV 136 reading and links the LMU discussion of its correction; it does not reproduce that obsolete reading. Selected IV and VI summaries still require modern critical collation.

Museum descriptions support object identity, material, catalogue dating and provenance as reported. Claims about function are labeled interpretation. A composition’s own claim of great age is a primary textual claim, not external confirmation of that age.

## Copyright and access

This repository contains brief original-language terms, original summaries, citations and bibliography metadata. It does not include complete modern translations, scanned books or downloaded museum images. A publicly accessible PDF is not thereby public domain. No copyright status should be inferred from where a file is hosted.

Before adding a continuous text or image, document the exact edition, rights holder, license, permissions and attribution. Prefer clearly reusable original-language editions, public-domain translations where applicable, and original project summaries. Keep short quotations within applicable limits and identify them explicitly. Never use the project license to relicense third-party material.

Original project code and original summaries use the root MIT license. Third-party linked texts, quotations and catalogues retain their rights. The preserved starter components and dependencies retain their upstream licenses.
